# Actor workers: attribution and engine comparison — 2026-10-24

Go 1.27.3, darwin/arm64, Apple M5 Max, GOMAXPROCS=8. Workloads and task
counts follow [the README](../README.md). Details and commits are in the
[environment record](2026-10-24-actor-environment.json).

**Noise.** Other processes loaded the host throughout, mainly repeatedly
restarted codebase-memory-mcp indexers that used up to about 16 cores in
bursts. The 1-minute load average was 4.97–6.04 during experiment A, about 5.2
during B, and 3.03–3.19 during C (5-minute: 6.62–6.93 and 5.50–5.63). Every
case therefore has 20 interleaved samples, and only large differences should be
read from these numbers. The profiles in B are short (0.4–0.8 s each).

## A: what made d810da1 faster

d810da1 made workers actors that receive drain and abort messages on their own
control channel instead of selecting on the master's shared `acceptDone`. The
`shared` tree adds that shared channel back to the worker's select
([patch](2026-10-24-actor-ablation-shared.patch)) and changes nothing else.
Order: before, after, shared, shared, after, before, run twice; medians in ns/task.

| Case | before (90ca605) | after (d810da1) | shared | shared vs after |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Noop, 1 worker | 393 | 229 | 359 | +57% (p<0.001) |
| Noop, 8 workers | 515 | 458 | 524 | +14% (p<0.001) |
| SHA2564KiB, 1 worker | 1,797 | 1,630 | 1,818 | +12% (p=0.010) |
| SHA2564KiB, 8 workers | 818 | 740 | 901 | +22% (p<0.001) |
| Wait100us, 1 worker | 119,500 | 119,400 | 119,700 | not significant |
| Wait100us, 8 workers | 14,920 | 14,960 | 14,940 | not significant |

`shared` is not significantly different from `before` except Noop with 8 workers
(+1.7%, p=0.007), so the speedup comes from no longer selecting on the shared
channel. Confidence intervals were within 10% except `before` SHA2564KiB with
8 workers (29%).

## B: where the time goes

CPU profiles of Noop, share of samples in `runtime.selectgo` and its lock
acquisition (`sellock`):

| Tree | Workers | selectgo | sellock |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| after | 1 | 13% (60 ms) | 11% |
| shared | 1 | 46% (320 ms) | 23% |
| after | 8 | 74% | 49% |
| shared | 8 | 61% | 46% |

With one worker, adding the shared channel quadruples the time spent in select,
mostly locking and unlocking channels. Submit's select also waits on
`acceptDone`, so the submitter and the worker contend for that lock; this
explanation fits the profile but was not isolated further. With eight workers,
both trees spend most of their time locking the channels every worker shares,
`direct` and the queue, which is the remaining bottleneck of the shared mailbox.

[shared, 1](2026-10-24-actor-profile-shared-w1.txt),
[shared, 8](2026-10-24-actor-profile-shared-w8.txt).

## C: engines at d810da1

Twenty rounds of `-count=1`, so the four engines alternate each round. Medians
in ns/task, with the 10th–90th percentile range for the pools.

| Case | channel | go-worker | ants | pond |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Noop, 1 worker | 22 | 243 (227–261) | 221 (208–229) | 368 (354–378) |
| Noop, 8 workers | 95 | 466 (448–752) | 412 (406–415) | 510 (495–531) |
| SHA2564KiB, 1 worker | 1,443 | 1,610 (1,549–1,697) | 1,855 (1,831–1,880) | 1,712 (1,638–1,741) |
| SHA2564KiB, 8 workers | 482 | 727 (698–964) | 766 (750–789) | 612 (602–630) |
| Wait100us, 1 worker | 119,584 | 119,900 | 119,904 | 119,757 |
| Wait100us, 8 workers | 14,910 | 14,918 | 14,952 | 14,940 |

go-worker uses 3 allocations and about 256 bytes per task. It is about 10%
slower than ants and 9–34% faster than pond on Noop, the fastest of the three
on SHA2564KiB with one worker, and between ants and pond with eight. Its
eight-worker samples have a wider upper tail than the other engines; that may
relate to the channel contention in B but was not verified.
